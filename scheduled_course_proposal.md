# Scheduled course work flow -


**1. Instructor sets up the course**
The instructor picks "create a scheduled course" instead of a regular course. As they build it, they fill in three things together: course metadata and the actual course content (sections, lectures, quizzes)[not required though], the schedule (when enrollment opens and closes, when the cohort starts and ends, how many seats), and — this is the new part — a rough outline of what's coming later, even if it's just section titles for weeks they haven't written yet or a course outline with topics that is going to be covered.

**2. Instructor submits for review**
One submit button. Behind the scenes it checks: does this have course outline, any content(optional), is the schedule's dates sensible (enrollment closes before the course starts, etc.), and is there a schedule at all. If anything's missing or a scheduled course has no schedule attached, it won't even let them submit — no more sneaking a half-built cohort course past the check.

**3. Admin reviews it — now with full context**
This is the actual fix. Right now the admin just sees "here's a course with full outline, looks fine." With the fix, the admin's screen also shows: this is a *scheduled* course, here's when it starts, here's how many weeks are planned versus how many actually have content. So instead of unknowingly rubber-stamping something that's going to drip-feed unreviewed material for three months, the admin makes an informed call — "yes, one believable outline is enough to approve," or "no, this looks too thin for a paid 12-week commitment."

**4. Admin approves → course goes live, schedule turns on by itself**
The moment the admin approves, two things happen automatically: the course becomes published, and its schedule flips from "draft" to "scheduled" — so the instructor doesn't have to remember a separate activation step. If the dates happen to have gone stale while it sat in review, the course still publishes, but the schedule stays off and the instructor gets a nudge to fix the dates and turn it on manually. Publishing the course is never held hostage by a scheduling hiccup.

**5. Enrollment window opens**
Learners can now see the course and enroll. If it's paid, they pay once for the course itself — that's separate from picking a specific cohort. If there's a seat cap, the last seat is handled safely so two people can't both grab it at once.

**6. The course starts**
On the start date, the system automatically flips the cohort to "ongoing." Before this moment, enrolled learners could see the outline but nothing was unlocked yet(if there are any contents) — no content leaks early.

**7. Instructor keeps adding material while it's running**
While the cohort is live, the instructor can upload contents — without needing another admin review each time. That part hasn't changed; it's still built on trust in the verified instructor. What *has* changed is that the admin already knew this was coming when they approved it, so it's no longer a surprise or a loophole.

**8. Content unlocks at instructor set time**
Each section or content can have its own unlock date. Learners see the whole outline the whole time, but locked sections or contents are greyed out with a "coming on this date" note, and any attempt to open them early gets a clear "not released yet" message instead of a confusing error.

**9. The cohort ends**
Once the end date passes, the instructor can't add or edit anything further and no new learners can join that run — but everyone who was in it keeps everything, forever. Nothing gets taken away.

**10. Reuse for next term**
The course itself doesn't get thrown away — the instructor can spin up a brand new schedule ("Spring 2027 Batch") on the same course content whenever they want to run it again.

The one meaningful change from today is step 3: the admin now sees the full picture — schedule and scope — before approving, instead of approving blind and finding out later what they actually signed off on.
